\begin{abstract}
A vehicle-to-vehicle cooperative perception system must decide, under a mean per-frame
communication budget, what its collaborator should transmit. Transmitting a feature map on every
frame costs $\FixedFOverBudgetRatio\times$ the budget; transmitting object-level detections on every
frame spends communication on frames whose detection would not change. We present \method{}, a
receiver-driven per-frame selector that chooses the transmitted representation from ego-local
perception cues and an estimated channel state, signals the choice with a two-bit request, and
charges every message through a measured LDPC/QAM chain.

At matched realised payload, \method{} exceeds a random selector, an ego-cue threshold and an SNR
threshold by \TestMPRandomDelta, \TestMPTaskOnlyDelta{} and \TestMPSnrOnlyDelta{} in scene-equal
$F_1$ on Test, with every pairwise $95\%$ scene-level bootstrap lower bound above zero; on
Culver-City it attains the highest point estimate and the interval against the SNR threshold
includes zero. Against always transmitting object-level detections it reduces
cooperative-perception payload by \TestSavingVsFixedL\% and \CulverSavingVsFixedL\% at scene-equal
$F_1$ differences of \TestDeltaFoneVsFixedL{} and \CulverDeltaFoneVsFixedL. Holding the number of
lost codewords fixed and varying which codewords are lost changes the detection outcome on
\PosIdealPct\% of matched pairs, so transport loss affects perception through its position and not
only its quantity. The contributions are the receiver-driven pre-request formulation, a per-frame
transport-accounting protocol, and these three measured results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{onnected} and automated vehicles exchange perception information over
vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) links to recover objects a single vehicle cannot see---at intersections, in
dense traffic, behind occlusions and at long range~\cite{xu2022opv2v,xu2022v2xvit}. What the
collaborator should transmit is not settled. Late fusion sends object-level
detections~\cite{xu2022opv2v}: compact, but discarding the intermediate representation. Intermediate
fusion sends feature maps~\cite{xu2022v2xvit,hu2022where2comm}: richer, at a payload that recent work
reduces by spatial selection, masking or compression~\cite{hu2022where2comm} and that semantic
communication optimises for task performance~\cite{sheng2024importance,gan2026scomcp}.

Two constraints bind that choice. First, a V2V link carries a mean payload budget: under the budget
used here, transmitting a feature map on every frame costs $\FixedFOverBudgetRatio\times$ what is
available. Second, the link is time-varying~\cite{ieee80211bd,3gpp37885}, so a message that would
help on a clean channel can arrive damaged, and the payload spent on it is wasted. The useful
representation therefore depends on both the perception state of the frame and the state of the
channel, and it is a per-frame quantity.

Existing methods select \emph{content} within a fixed representation and are evaluated against
declared payload budgets. Two gaps follow. A method that varies the representation itself needs
inputs available \emph{before} the message is requested, or the decision depends on the message it
is deciding about. And a payload claim needs the transmitted bits charged through the transport
chain they traverse, or the comparison rests on a constant that no measurement produced.

This paper addresses both. \method{} forms a $\CueDim$-dimensional input from $21$ ego-local
perception and collaborator-availability cues and $2$ channel cues, predicts a communication action
with a random forest, and encodes the action as a two-bit request; the collaborator returns the
requested representation and the ego fuses it. Every payload is charged per frame through a measured
LDPC/QAM chain. The candidate action set is $\mathcal{A} = \{E, L, F\}$---ego-only, object-level and
feature-level---and the policy frozen on the development split selects E or L.

Our contributions are three.
\begin{itemize}
\item A receiver-driven per-frame selection formulation in which every selector input is ego-local
and exists before the request is issued, so no input depends on the message being requested. The
objective is the Lagrangian relaxation of a budget-constrained perception--communication problem
(Section~\ref{sec:derivation}), with the budget a constraint on the mean payload.
\item A per-frame transport-accounting protocol: each message is packetised, coded and modulated,
and charged in channel uses, so payload comparisons are measured rather than declared
(Section~\ref{sec:communication_cost}).
\item Three measured results under one detector, one field of view and one ground truth: at matched
realised payload \method{} exceeds random, ego-cue and SNR-threshold selection on Test
(Section~\ref{sec:matched}); it reduces cooperative-perception payload against always transmitting
object-level detections (Section~\ref{sec:matched}); and loss \emph{position} at fixed loss
\emph{amount} changes the detection outcome (Section~\ref{sec:transport}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{V2V Cooperative Perception}
V2V cooperative perception exploits information from surrounding connected vehicles to alleviate
occlusion, long-range sparsity and limited field of view~\cite{xu2022opv2v}. Methods are categorised
by the stage at which information is shared. \emph{Early fusion} shares raw point clouds before
feature extraction~\cite{xu2022opv2v}: the most complete information, but a very large payload
requiring accurate spatio-temporal alignment. \emph{Late fusion} shares object-level
predictions---boxes, classes, confidences~\cite{xu2022opv2v}. The payload is small and suits
low-rate links, but intermediate semantic and spatial information is discarded: an object missed by
the sender cannot be recovered by the receiver. \emph{Intermediate fusion} shares learned
bird's-eye-view (BEV) features between backbone and detection
head~\cite{xu2022v2xvit,hu2022where2comm,wang2020v2vnet,xu2022cobevt}, balancing the two extremes at
a much larger message size. Benchmarks such as OPV2V and OpenCOOD~\cite{xu2022opv2v} provide common
datasets and protocols for comparing these strategies.

\emph{Relation to \method{}.} These works fix the representation and study fusion within it.
\method{} controls whether object-level cooperation is requested on each frame. Feature-level
transmission is retained in the unified evaluation framework as a fixed-action upper bound and
transport-analysis branch, but is not activated by the frozen deployed policy.

\subsection{Communication-Efficient Cooperative Perception}
To reduce the communication burden of intermediate fusion, many methods transmit only task-relevant
features, using confidence maps, attention weights or importance masks to select informative spatial
regions~\cite{hu2022where2comm,sheng2024importance,li2021disconet,liu2020when2com}.
Where2comm~\cite{hu2022where2comm} is representative: it estimates where communication is needed and
selectively transmits spatial feature regions. Feature compression is a second direction, encoding
the map into a lower-dimensional representation before
transmission~\cite{sheng2024importance,zhang2024smartcooper}. ML-Cooper~\cite{xie2022mlcooper} adapts
the proportion of raw-, feature- and object-level data to the network state with a soft
actor--critic policy, and RBH~\cite{liu2024rbh} chooses hybrid fusion by spatial overlap.

\emph{Relation to \method{}.} Where2comm selects \emph{which spatial positions} to send inside the
feature level; importance-map and JSCC methods optimise \emph{how} the feature map is encoded and
carried over the channel. ML-Cooper is the
closest in spirit, in that it mixes levels, but it does so as a heavyweight learned resource-sharing
policy with a different action mechanism. \method{} acts one level above the question those methods
answer: it controls whether object-level cooperation is requested on each frame. F is retained only
as the upper-bound and transport-analysis branch described above.

\subsection{Semantic and Channel-Aware Communication for Vehicular Perception}
Semantic communication optimises the transmitted representation for task performance rather than for
reconstruction fidelity or bit error
rate~\cite{sheng2024importance,gan2026scomcp,bourtsoulatze2019deepjscc,xie2021deepsc,gunduz2023beyond}.
Joint source--channel coding trains encoder and decoder with the channel in the loop and can degrade
more gracefully than separated source and channel coding, whereas digital QAM$+$LDPC transport shows
threshold behaviour~\cite{ieee80211bd}: poor below an SNR and rapidly reliable above it. That
dependence matters most for vehicular links, whose SNR varies with multipath, blockage and
fading~\cite{3gpp37885}. Some work makes the transmission channel-adaptive at a \emph{fixed}
granularity: SmartCooper~\cite{zhang2024smartcooper} adjusts the compression ratio from the estimated
channel state, and AccBEV~\cite{accbev2025} conditions a feature-repair module on the SNR.
CoDS~\cite{gan2025cods} pairs a task-oriented semantic codec with a semantic analog-to-digital
converter so that learned features traverse a standard digital V2X bitstream, and discards features
corrupted by decoding failures at the receiver. Sheng \emph{et al.}~\cite{sheng2026tccn} train the
feature encoder and decoder jointly with the channel so that the transmitted representation degrades
gracefully; that codec would occupy the feature branch this paper measures, whereas \method{}
decides whether the branch is requested.

\emph{Relation to \method{}.} All of these adapt the encoding, the repair or the rate \emph{within}
feature-level transmission. \method{} acts before that: it controls whether object-level
cooperation is requested on each frame. F is retained only as the upper-bound and
transport-analysis branch described above. The two lines of work are therefore complementary rather
than competing: a digital semantic codec would occupy the feature branch this paper measures as an
upper bound.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Cooperative Perception Setting}
We consider a single ego--collaborator V2V link. At frame $t$ the ego observes the scene with its
own LiDAR and extracts a local BEV feature $F_{\mathrm{ego},t}$ with a PointPillars
backbone~\cite{lang2019pointpillars}; the collaborator observes from its own viewpoint and extracts
$F_{j,t}$ with the \emph{same} checkpoint. The ego fuses whatever it receives with its local feature
and produces the final 3D detection $\hat Y_t$, which is scored against the ground truth $Y_t$.

We adopt a \emph{receiver-driven} architecture: the ego evaluates its own perception and channel
state, decides which granularity it needs, and signals the choice with a $2$-bit request. We treat
the request as an application-layer extension associated with the ETSI Cooperative Awareness
Message~\cite{etsi_cam} and Collective Perception Message~\cite{etsi_cpm} services, carried over
IEEE~802.11bd or NR sidelink~\cite{ieee80211bd}; the standards integration itself is outside the
scope of this paper. \textbf{The request is a fixed control overhead, identical for all three
actions, and is therefore not charged to any action's payload.} It is sent on every frame, including
when the ego selects E; what E removes is the cooperative perception payload, not the request.

\subsection{Message Candidates}\label{sec:candidates}
The ego selects one action per frame,
\begin{equation}
s_t \in \mathcal{A} = \{E, L, F\},
\label{eq:action_set}
\end{equation}
where E is ego-only operation---the collaborator transmits no cooperative perception payload and the
ego detects from its own LiDAR---L is the collaborator's detected boxes, and F is the collaborator's
quantised bottleneck feature tensor.

The candidate action set is $\mathcal{A} = \{E, L, F\}$. The frozen policy selected during the
development protocol has support $\{E, L\}$; feature-level transmission remains in the candidate
set and in the unified evaluation framework.

F's payload is $\FixedFOverLPayRatio\times$ that of L on the development split, so Fixed F is
infeasible as an always-on policy, sitting at $\FixedFOverBudgetRatio\times$ the primary
budget. F is measured and charged through the identical chain throughout, as an over-budget
fixed-action reference and as the object of the transport study of Section~\ref{sec:transport}.

E transmits no cooperative payload and cannot fail in transit.

The object-level message is a compact set of local detections,
$m_t^{L} = \{(b_k, c_k, p_k)\}_{k=1}^{N_t}$, with $b_k$ the 3D box, $c_k$ the class and $p_k$ the
confidence. The feature-level message is the encoder's bottleneck tensor, $\BFelements$ elements,
quantised to $w = \QuantWidth$~bits per element before transmission. \textbf{No modulation-order
variant is part of the action space:} the selector chooses \emph{what to send}, whereas the
constellation is a transport parameter of the link.

\subsection{Channel Model and Transport}\label{sec:channel}
Let $\gamma_t$ be the estimated channel quality at frame $t$ in dB, available from pilot-based
estimation or link-layer reports as already produced by IEEE~802.11bd and 5G NR sidelink
stacks~\cite{ieee80211bd}. Messages are carried by a rate-$1/2$ LDPC code with information block
$K = \LdpcK$ and codeword length $n = \LdpcN$, modulated with $\QamM$-QAM.

A message is fragmented into packets of $\PacketBits$ payload bits with a $\HeaderBits$-bit header
following common V2X framing assumptions~\cite{ieee80211bd,3gpp37885}, and each packet is coded into
LDPC codewords. Each codeword succeeds or fails independently at the frame's operating point with
per-codeword loss probability $p$. Three delivery regimes are evaluated:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{fragment-aware partial recovery} (the primary regime for the feature message): the
receiver reconstructs the tensor from the codewords that arrive and \emph{zero-fills} the elements
carried by codewords that do not;
\item \textbf{packet loss}: the same rule applied at packet granularity, a post-freeze transport
sensitivity;
\item \textbf{message all-or-nothing}: the message is usable only if all of its codewords decode, a
pessimistic sensitivity arm for the feature message.
\end{itemize}

Under partial recovery, let $M_t \in \{0,1\}^{N_{\mathrm{cw}}}$ mark the codewords that decode and
let $E \in \{0,1\}^{N \times N_{\mathrm{cw}}}$ map each codeword to the tensor elements it carries.
The receiver reconstructs
\begin{equation}
\hat F_{j,t} = \mathrm{Dequant}\bigl(q_{j,t} \odot (E M_t)\bigr),
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
where $q_{j,t}$ is the quantised tensor and $\odot$ is elementwise. Elements carried by a failed
codeword take the dequantised zero code point, which is exact for a symmetric int8 code.

The delivery semantics of the two cooperative actions are not the same, and the distinction is
load-bearing:

\begin{quote}
Late fusion uses message-level delivery. For frame $t$, all $N_{\mathrm{cw},L,t}$ codewords must be
received for the collaborator box message to be available, giving
$q_{L,t} = (1-p)^{N_{\mathrm{cw},L,t}}$; otherwise execution falls back to the ego-only result. In
contrast, the primary feature-fusion regime performs codeword-level partial recovery and does not
fall back to ego-only when only part of the feature message is lost.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Effective Task Utility under Channel Corruption}\label{sec:effective_utility}
Let $F_t^{s}$ denote the realised frame $F_1$ when action $s$ is executed at frame
$t$.\footnote{Here, $F_t^{s}$ denotes the frame-level $F_1$ utility under action
$s \in \{E, L, F\}$. Thus, $F_t^{F}$ is the frame-level $F_1$ obtained when the feature-level
action $F$ is executed.} For the
object-level action the message-level rule above gives
\begin{align}
F_t^{L}(p) &= q_{L,t}\,F_t^{L,\mathrm{clean}} + \bigl(1 - q_{L,t}\bigr) F_t^{\mathrm{ego}},
\notag \\
q_{L,t} &= (1-p)^{N_{\mathrm{cw},L,t}} ,
\label{eq:eff_L}
\end{align}
where $F_t^{\mathrm{ego}}$ is the ego-only frame $F_1$. For the feature-level action under partial
recovery there is no such mixture, because a partly damaged tensor is still fused: $F_t^{F}(p)$ is
\emph{measured} on the reconstructed tensor at each sampled $p$ and interpolated piecewise-linearly
in raw $p$ between the sampled nodes, with the endpoints locked to the clean measurement at $p = 0$
and the all-lost measurement at $p = 1$. No monotone correction is applied, because some masks raise
a frame's $F_1$ by removing false positives and smoothing that away would erase a measured effect
(Section~\ref{sec:transport}).

Payload accounting is separate from this expectation: \textbf{communication is
charged for the message that is attempted and is never multiplied by the success probability.} A
failed delivery does not refund symbols already spent on the channel.

\subsection{Communication Cost}\label{sec:communication_cost}
Payload is a per-frame quantity, not a constant per action:
\begin{equation}
B_{t,s} =
\begin{cases}
0, & s = E \text{ (or no collaborator)},\\
B_{L,t}, & s = L,\\
B_F, & s = F,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:payload}
\end{equation}
in channel-use-equivalent mega-symbols (Msym). \textbf{$B_E = 0$ is the cooperative perception
payload, not the total channel use of the frame}: the request itself is a fixed control overhead
that every action pays identically (Section~\ref{sec:system}), so it cancels from absolute payload
differences; the
reported relative savings refer to cooperative-perception payload only. It is excluded from
$B_{t,s}$ throughout. The feature payload follows from the
codeword count
rather than from $(\text{info} + \text{header})/R/\log_2 M$, because the direct route ignores
codeword padding: $\BFelements$ elements at $\QuantWidth$~bits give $\BFinfobits$ information bits,
which packetise into $\BFpackets$ packets carrying $\BFheaderbits$ header bits, hence
$N_{\mathrm{cw}} = \BFncw$ codewords and $B_F = \BFmsym$~Msym per frame. The direct route would have
given $\BFdirectmsym$~Msym, a difference that is pure padding. The object-level payload is charged
through the identical chain from each frame's own box count, at $\BoxBits$~bits per box, ranging
from $\BLmin$ to $\BLmax$~Msym, i.e.\ $\NcwLmin$ to $\NcwLmax$ codewords. On the development
split, the collaborator transmits an average of $\NboxCollabMean$ boxes per frame, producing $\NcwLmean$ LDPC codewords and
$\BLmean$~Msym. The post-fusion late-detection output contains $\NboxLateOutputMean$ boxes on
average, but this value is not used for payload accounting.

The mean cost over $T$ frames is $\bar B = (1/T)\sum_t B_{t,s_t}$. Table~\ref{tab:payload_chain}

\subsection{Task--Communication Objective}
Let $z_t$ be the ego-side cue vector and $(\hat\gamma_t, c_t)$ the estimated channel state. The
selector is a map
\begin{equation}
s_t = g(z_t, \hat\gamma_t, c_t).
\label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Derivation: Constrained Optimisation and Rate--Distortion View}\label{sec:derivation}
The design problem is
\begin{equation}
\max_{g}\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[F_t^{s_t}\right]
\quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{E}\!\left[B_{t,s_t}\right] \le \beta B_F ,
\label{eq:constrained}
\end{equation}
where the budget is expressed \emph{normalised} to the feature payload. We retain the three budget
tiers $\beta \in \{\BudgetTightBeta, \BudgetPrimaryBeta, \BudgetLooseBeta\}$, i.e.
$\BudgetTightMsym$, $\BudgetPrimaryMsym$ and $\BudgetLooseMsym$~Msym per frame; the primary cell is
$\beta = \BudgetPrimaryBeta$. \textbf{The budget is a mean constraint across frames, not a per-frame
cap}---under a per-frame cap any $\beta < 1$ would make F unselectable on every frame and the problem
would degenerate.

Introducing a multiplier $\lambda \ge 0$ gives the pointwise relaxed optimum
\begin{equation}
s_t^{\star}(\lambda) = \arg\max_{s \in \mathcal{A}} \bigl(F_t^{s} - \lambda B_{t,s}\bigr),
\label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}
with ties broken $E \succ L \succ F$. The learned selector is trained against
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagrangian}, and the constrained problem is addressed by sweeping $\lambda$ over the
family.

In rate--distortion terms, Eq.~\eqref{eq:eff_L} and the measured $F_t^{F}(p)$ express a
channel-induced distortion that grows with the loss rate, while payload is the rate. The selector
therefore trades rate against rate-conditional distortion as a function of channel state---joint
source--channel structure expressed at the granularity-action level rather than at the symbol level.
This yields the design principle the paper rests on: \emph{the most informative message is not always
the most useful message under communication constraints.}

\section{Proposed Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
Instead of transmitting a fixed representation on every frame, the ego constructs an ego-local cue
vector, reads its channel estimate, runs a lightweight selector, signals the chosen granularity with
the $2$-bit request, and the collaborator complies. Fig.~\ref{fig:system} shows the data flow as
implemented, including which quantities exist before the request is issued.

\subsection{Ego-Side Online Task Cues}\label{sec:cues}
The selector input is the concatenation of three ego-local groups and the channel state,
\begin{equation}
x_t = \bigl[\,\underbrace{p_t}_{19},\; \underbrace{n_t}_{1},\; \underbrace{a_t}_{1},\;
\underbrace{\hat\gamma_t,\, c_t}_{2}\,\bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{\CueDim},
\label{eq:cue}
\end{equation}
where $p_t$ are $19$ statistics of the ego's own LiDAR sweep (point counts, range statistics,
near/mid/far and per-sector occupancies, and three range densities), $n_t$ is the ego's own detected
box count, $a_t \in \{0,1\}$ indicates collaborator availability, $\hat\gamma_t$ is the estimated
SNR in dB and $c_t \in \{0,1\}$ the channel-type indicator. Table~\ref{tab:cues_main} lists the
frozen schema \CueName{} field by field.

\emph{Every cue is computed from the ego vehicle's own observation and its own detection stream}, and
every field is computable before the request is issued. This is a direct consequence of the
receiver-driven architecture: nothing in $z_t$ depends on a message the ego has not yet asked for.
Ground truth, task metrics, oracle labels and delivery outcomes are excluded from the schema by
construction, and the cue schema is verified against its code-defined sources.

\subsection{Channel Quality Cue}
The channel state is the estimated SNR $\hat\gamma_t \in \mathbb{R}$ in dB and a binary channel-type
indicator $c_t$. In a deployed V2X stack $\hat\gamma_t$ comes from pilot-symbol estimation already
produced by 802.11bd or NR sidelink receivers~\cite{ieee80211bd} and $c_t$ can be inferred from
link-layer multipath indicators. Both are treated as \emph{estimates}, and the results below are
conditional on their being available at the ego.

The role of $(\hat\gamma_t, c_t)$ differs from that of the task cues. Task cues estimate whether the
frame \emph{needs} richer information; channel cues estimate whether such information \emph{can be
carried}. A difficult frame does not by itself justify a feature request.

\subsection{Message Branches}
The object-level branch transmits the collaborator's detected boxes and is charged per frame from its
own box count. The feature-level branch transmits the encoder's bottleneck tensor quantised to
$\QuantWidth$~bits per element. Quantisation is part of the transmitted form and its cost is
measured: on a $\IntEightFrames$-frame audit subsample of the development split the mean
frame $F_1$ moves from $\IntEightFloatFone$ (float) to $\IntEightFone$ (int8), a difference of
$\IntEightDeltaFone$. This is a quantisation audit on that subsample and is not a clean-channel
result for the full split.

\subsection{Channel-Aware Semantic Granularity Selector}\label{sec:selector}
The policy $g(\cdot)$ is instantiated as a random forest~\cite{breiman2001random} with
$\SelectorTrees$ trees and a minimum of $\SelectorLeaf$ samples per leaf, operating on the
$\CueDim$ tabular features of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cue}. The frozen forest holds $\SelectorNodes$ nodes
of which $\SelectorLeaves$ are leaves, with a realised maximum depth of $\SelectorMaxDepth$ and a
serialised size of $\SelectorSizeMB$~MB. It adds no learnable parameters to the perception backbone and requires no retraining of the
detector, which is why a tabular learner is a natural fit; \emph{the forest is one realisation of the
selection layer, not the contribution.}

The selector is trained to imitate the utility-maximising action of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagrangian}. That
action uses realised task utility and is not deployable; it supplies labels only. Writing
$s_t^{\star}(\lambda)$ for the label of grid row $t$, the forest minimises the class-weighted
misclassification risk
\begin{equation}
\min_{g}\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} w_{s_t^{\star}(\lambda)}\,
\mathbf{1}\bigl[g(x_t) \neq s_t^{\star}(\lambda)\bigr],
\label{eq:train}
\end{equation}
with $w$ the inverse class frequencies of the label set and $x_t$ the input of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cue}.

\paragraph*{Feasibility} \textbf{Mainline feasibility is structural.} An action is feasible iff a
collaborator is available and a transport response is defined; on a frame with no collaborator the
executed action is forced to E. We do not apply a message-level BLER feasibility mask because the
quantity it thresholds is the per-codeword erasure probability rather than the probability that the
feature message is unusable, and because channel impairment is already reflected in the
partial-recovery effectiveness. The feature output is defined and measured at every loss rate,
including $p = 1$. No $F_t^{F} - F_t^{\mathrm{ego}}$ safety threshold is added:
with $\lambda \ge 0$ and $B_F > 0$, $F_t^{F} \le F_t^{\mathrm{ego}}$ implies $U_F \le U_E$, so the
objective already excludes those rows.

\subsection{Receiver-Side Fusion and Detection}
The ego fuses the received message with its local feature and detects,
$\hat Y_t = D\bigl(\Psi(F_{\mathrm{ego},t}, m_t^{s_t})\bigr)$, with $D$ the detection head. For
$s_t = F$, $\Psi$ is the attentive fusion of the backbone: at each BEV cell the ego feature and the
received feature form a two-element sequence that attends over itself,
\begin{align}
\Psi(F_{\mathrm{ego},t}, \hat F_{j,t})
&= \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V, \notag \\
Q = K = V &= \bigl[\,F_{\mathrm{ego},t};\; \hat F_{j,t}\,\bigr],
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{align}
where $d$ is the channel dimension and $\hat F_{j,t}$ is the reconstruction of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recon}. Because the softmax is taken over both elements, a received tensor of zeros
still contributes weight. For $s_t = L$ the receiver performs object-level fusion of the received
boxes with its own; for $s_t = E$ it detects from $F_{\mathrm{ego},t}$ alone.

\textbf{A zeroed collaborator tensor is not the same input as no collaborator at all.} The fusion
module attends over both branches, so a zero tensor participates and dilutes the ego features. The
difference is measured in Section~\ref{sec:transport}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Per-frame operation at inference}
\label{alg:infer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State ego computes $p_t, n_t, a_t$ from its own sweep and detections
\State ego reads $\hat\gamma_t, c_t$ from the receiver's link estimate
\State $x_t \gets [\,p_t, n_t, a_t, \hat\gamma_t, c_t\,]$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:cue}}
\State $s_t \gets g(x_t)$ \Comment{frozen selector}
\If{$a_t = 0$}
  \State $s_t \gets E$ \Comment{structural: no collaborator}
\EndIf
\State ego transmits $s_t$ as a two-bit request
\If{$s_t \neq E$}
  \State collaborator packetises, codes and modulates $m_t^{s_t}$; charge $B_{t,s_t}$
  \State ego reconstructs by Eq.~\eqref{eq:recon} from the codewords that decode
\EndIf
\State $\hat Y_t \gets D(\Psi(F_{\mathrm{ego},t}, m_t^{s_t}))$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Implementation}
We evaluate on the OPV2V dataset~\cite{xu2022opv2v} using the OpenCOOD codebase, with a PointPillars
BEV backbone~\cite{lang2019pointpillars}. \textbf{One checkpoint serves all three actions}, and the
field of view and ground truth are identical across them, so an $L$-versus-$F$ comparison is a
comparison of granularity rather than of two separately trained pipelines. Exactly one collaborator
is used, selected by a fixed rule, so the payload of a cooperative action is well defined.

In the evaluation, collaborator availability is determined before action selection from the frame's
vehicle identities, relative poses and the fixed communication-range rule. In deployment,
equivalent neighbour identity and position information can be obtained from periodic
cooperative-awareness broadcasts; determining availability therefore does not require receiving the
requested perception payload.

Evaluation is deterministic: the per-sample point shuffle is seeded on
$(\text{split}, \text{scene}, \text{frame}, \text{cav})$, and two independent processes reproduce a
run bit for bit, verified by a reconstruction-consistency bridge.

\subsection{Selector Training and Freezing}\label{sec:training}
Model selection is done \textbf{on the development split only}, in this order.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Scene-level $9$-fold leave-one-scene-out (LOSO) on validate.} Folds are scene-disjoint,
because frames within a scene are consecutive samples of the same traffic and are far from
independent.
\item \emph{Candidate and penalty selection.} A candidate walk over selector configurations and
penalties $\lambda$ is run under the budget constraint, scored by LOSO.
\item \emph{Refit and freeze.} The selected configuration---candidate \CandIdx{} at
$\lambda = \LambdaStar$---is refitted on the full development split and frozen, together with the
comparator: an SNR threshold at $\tau = \TauStar$~dB, the best budget-feasible threshold at the
primary cell. Per-tier thresholds are $\TauTight$, $\TauPrimary$ and $\TauLoose$~dB for
$\beta = \BudgetTightBeta$, $\BudgetPrimaryBeta$ and $\BudgetLooseBeta$.
\item \emph{One-shot held-out evaluation.} Test and Culver-City are evaluated only after model
and comparator freezing.
\end{enumerate}

LOSO is the \emph{training-time} selection procedure and the scene-level bootstrap of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics} is the \emph{evaluation-time} interval procedure; they operate at different
stages and neither replaces the other. Algorithm~\ref{alg:train} states the procedure.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Selector selection and freezing (development split)}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require grid rows $\{(x_t, F_t^{E}, F_t^{L}, F_t^{F}, B_{t,\cdot})\}$, scenes $\mathcal{S}$,
  candidates $\mathcal{C}$, penalties $\Lambda$, budget $\beta$
\ForAll{$(c, \lambda) \in \mathcal{C} \times \Lambda$}
  \State label each row by $s_t^{\star}(\lambda)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagrangian}
  \ForAll{scenes $u \in \mathcal{S}$}
    \State fit $g_{c}$ on $\mathcal{S} \setminus \{u\}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:train}; predict on $u$
  \EndFor
  \State record held-out-fold utility and mean payload $\bar B(c,\lambda)$
\EndFor
\State $(c^{\star}, \lambda^{\star}) \gets$ best utility among $\{\bar B(c,\lambda) \le \beta B_F\}$
\State refit $g_{c^{\star}}$ on all scenes; \textbf{freeze} $g$, $\lambda^{\star}$ and the
  comparator $\tau^{\star}$
\State \Return frozen $g$ \Comment{held-out splits are opened only after this point}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Baselines}
\textbf{Fixed E}, \textbf{Fixed L} and \textbf{Fixed F} bound the action space. The internal decision
rules are the \textbf{SNR-threshold rule} and \textbf{two- and three-scalar hand rules} over the same
cues, fitted through the identical LOSO walk and budget constraint. The \textbf{budget-blind oracle}
selects the utility-maximising action per row and is an upper-bound reference, not a deployable
policy. The frozen $\tau = \TauStar$ is the primary comparator because it is the only arm sharing
\method{}'s complete payload definition.

\textbf{Where2comm} is included as an external feature-content
baseline~\cite{hu2022where2comm}. It is evaluated under the same data splits, field of view, ground
truth and detection metrics. We report
its native communication rate because the evaluated implementation transmits floating-point selected
features and does not execute the locked int8 quantisation path. Consequently, it is excluded from
bit-level budget-matched claims. No adaptive-policy baseline met the reproducibility bar for
inclusion as a policy comparator.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}
AP@0.5 and AP@0.7 characterise the fixed perception arms, transport responses and the external
Where2comm reference. The confirmatory comparison between the frozen selector and its
budget-feasible comparator uses scene-equal mean per-frame $F_1$, with scene-level bootstrap
intervals. Communication cost is the mean per-frame channel use defined in
Section~\ref{sec:communication_cost}.

The non-inferiority margin is $\delta = \DeltaMargin$ in absolute mean per-frame $F_1$. It is a
\emph{preregistered engineering tolerance}---how much accuracy may be given up in exchange for a
substantive communication reduction---fixed on 2026-08-09, before any evaluation on either held-out
split. \textbf{It is not a safety-certified threshold}, and no statistical or regulatory standard is
claimed for its value. \textbf{The confirmatory unit is the scene, not the
frame.} The headline statistic is therefore the scene-equal mean $F_1$, and intervals are
scene-level bootstraps with \TestScenes{} Test scenes and \CulverScenes{} Culver-City scenes,
covering \TestRows{} and \CulverRows{} evaluated rows. Every payload figure is reported under two
accountings---over all frames, and over frames where a collaborator is available---and the two are
never mixed.

The frozen selector requests E on \TestRhoEPct\% of Test rows and L on \TestRhoLPct\%, and never

\subsection{Transport Mechanism}\label{sec:transport}
Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} and the full sweep in the supplementary material characterise the three
delivery regimes.

\emph{Partial recovery is necessary, not a refinement} (development split). Under all-or-nothing
message delivery the
survival probability of a $\BFncw$-codeword message is $q = \MsgSurvivalAtLowest$ even at the
\emph{lowest} evaluated loss rate $p = \MsgLowestRate$, i.e. an expectation of
$\MsgFramesAtLowest$ surviving frames over the whole development split. That regime therefore
measures $q$, not how the feature action performs, and any conclusion drawn from it would be a
conclusion about message length.

\emph{Where a loss falls matters, at fixed loss amount} (development split). Conditioning on
\emph{exactly equal}
codeword-loss counts across replicates, $\Delta F_1 \neq 0$ on \PosIdealPct\% of matched pairs under
fragment-aware recovery (\PosIdealPairs{} pairs over \PosIdealFrames{} frames; Wilson 95\% lower
bound \PosIdealWilsonLo\%) and on \PosPacketPct\% under packet loss (\PosPacketPairs{} pairs,
\PosPacketFrames{} frames, lower bound \PosPacketWilsonLo\%). The sufficiency rule for this test was
fixed before the selection ran. No threshold for position-versus-amount was preregistered, so only
the weaker statement is made: loss position affects task performance.

\emph{Zero-tensor fusion is not ego-only inference} (development split). At $p = 1$ every codeword
is lost and the fused
tensor is all zeros, yet the result differs from ego-only detection in both directions:
AP@0.5 moves by $\ZeroTensorMinusEgoAPFifty$, AP@0.7 by $\ZeroTensorMinusEgoAPSeventy$ and mean frame
$F_1$ by $\ZeroTensorMinusEgoMeanFOne$ (from $\EgoFone$ to $\ZeroTensorFone$), and the two outputs
have identical boxes on $\ZeroTensorIdenticalFrames$ of the development split's frames. \textbf{The
signs disagree across metrics}, so the two states are not interchangeable in an evaluation that
models delivery failure.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

At matched realised payload the channel state carries most of the decision: the SNR-threshold rule
reaches $\TestMPSnrOnlyFone$ on Test and $\CulverMPSnrOnlyFone$ on Culver-City against \method{}'s
$\TestMPCaTosgFone$ and $\CulverMPCaTosgFone$. The two arms differ in model class as well as in
inputs---a single threshold against a random forest---so the difference between them is not
attributable to the ego-local cues alone.

All three budget tiers select the same candidate and the budget never binds, so this study does not
separate three operating points. Under the same budget ceiling the two feasible policies occupy
different points: the threshold rule attains higher scene-equal $F_1$ and \method{} spends less
realised payload.

\subsection{Why the Frozen Policy Operates over E/L}
\label{sec:no_feature}
Fixed F costs $\FixedFOverLPayRatio\times$ the object-level message ($\BFmsym$~Msym against
$\BLmean$~Msym on the development split), which is $\FixedFOverBudgetRatio\times$ the
$\beta = \BudgetPrimaryBeta$ ceiling of $\BudgetPrimaryMsym$~Msym. Because the budget constrains the
mean payload rather than each frame (Section~\ref{sec:derivation}), that arithmetic excludes F as an
always-on policy but not as an occasional request. F is the utility-maximising action on
\FBestSharePct\% of development-split grid rows (\FBestRows{} rows), exceeding E and L there by a
mean net gain of $\FBestNetGain$, against which the frozen penalty charges
$\lambda B_F = \LambdaTimesBF$~Msym---a penalty-to-gain ratio of $\PenaltyOverGainRatio$. The
break-even penalty is $\BreakEvenCond$ conditional on the rows where F is best and $\BreakEvenGrid$
averaged over the whole grid, each value carrying its conditioning set; both fall between two points
of the preregistered penalty grid, so no candidate on that grid selects F. A finer scan of that
interval is an exploratory development-split diagnostic reported in the supplementary material; it
does not establish how a retrained selector would behave in the break-even region.

\subsection{Other Limitations}
\emph{Culver-City rests on \CulverScenes{} scenes.} The bootstrap resamples them, so its interval is
coarse, and Culver-City is a \emph{simulated} domain shift rather than real-road data.

\emph{One ego--collaborator pair.} The payload of a cooperative action is defined against exactly one
collaborator; a deployment serving several would have to re-derive both the payload and the budget.

\emph{An independent codeword-erasure abstraction.} Each codeword succeeds or fails independently
at the frame's operating point; correlated fading, burst erasures and imperfect channel estimation
are not modelled.

\emph{The recovery asymmetry between L and F is a modelling choice.} The object-level message is
delivered at message level, so a single lost codeword costs the whole message, whereas the
feature message is recovered codeword by codeword. That asymmetry follows from how each message is
framed here, not from a property of object-level data: encoding the boxes as independently
decodable groups would make L degrade gracefully too, which would change its effective utility, the
oracle labels derived from it, and therefore the selector's decisions. That transport design is not
evaluated in this paper.

\emph{Selector-only latency.} On one CPU core the frozen forest predicts a single frame in
$\SelectorLatencyMed$~ms (5th--95th percentile $\SelectorLatencyLo$--$\SelectorLatencyHi$~ms over
$\LatencyTrials$ trials), and the point statistics that build the cue vector take
$\CueLatencyMed$~ms. Total end-to-end system latency, including any cost of keeping the cooperative
pipeline resident, is not measured.

\emph{One dataset family and one detector.} Every result here is conditional on OPV2V and on a single
PointPillars checkpoint.

\emph{A collaborator-availability mismatch across splits.} The development split contains a
collaborator on every frame, whereas \TestNoCollab\% of Test frames and \CulverNoCollab\% of
Culver-City frames do not. These rows do not test learned action selection because execution is
structurally forced to E and all cooperative actions have zero realised payload. Nevertheless, the
difference limits interpretation of the cross-domain action proportions.

\section{Conclusion}
Three transmission granularities were placed under one detector, one field of view and one ground
truth, and charged through one measured transport chain. Under the mean per-frame communication budget, the
frozen policy operates over ego-only and object-level actions, and \method{} makes that choice per
frame from ego-local cues and an estimated channel state; feature-level transmission is retained as
an over-budget upper bound.

In a secondary analysis at matched realised payload, \method{} achieves a higher scene-equal $F_1$
than each evaluated deployable comparator on Test, with every pairwise bound above zero, and the
highest point estimate on Culver-City, where the interval against the SNR-only rule includes zero.
Against the frozen threshold comparator communication falls by a factor of \TestRatio{} on Test and
\CulverRatio{} on Culver-City, while the preregistered accuracy non-inferiority is not established
on either. The conditions are one dataset family, one detector, a single ego--collaborator pair,
and an independent codeword-erasure abstraction.

The mechanism results are the part most likely to transfer. Partial recovery is not an optimisation
but a precondition for studying feature-level transport at realistic message lengths; loss position
changes the task outcome at fixed loss amount, so a transport model that tracks only how much was
lost is incomplete; and a zeroed collaborator tensor is not an ego-only input, so the two must be
distinguished in any evaluation that models delivery failure. Each of these is a statement about the
transport--perception interface rather than about one policy, and each was measured under a protocol
in which the detector, the field of view and the ground truth were held fixed.

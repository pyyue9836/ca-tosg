\section{The frozen cue schema}
Table~\ref{tab:cues} lists all $\CueDim$ fields of the frozen schema \CueName{} in order, with the
code location each is computed at. Two properties hold of the schema by construction.

First, \emph{every field is ego-local}: it is computed from the ego vehicle's own LiDAR sweep or its
own detection output, so the vector exists before the request is issued and no field depends on a
message the ego has not yet asked for.

Second, \emph{no ground truth, task metric, oracle label, delivery outcome or future information
reaches the schema}. The cue schema is verified against its code-defined
sources, not by name matching---a wrong implementation would also rename its fields, so a name check
would prove nothing.

The field of view over which the statistics are defined, the point source, and the detector
checkpoint the box count depends on are all pinned in the schema manifest, because a schema frozen
without pinning its inputs would be frozen against a moving input.

\section{Payload derivation, bit by bit}
The feature message is the encoder's bottleneck tensor. Every step below is a counted quantity, not
a declared convention.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Elements.} A dummy forward through the deployed checkpoint gives $\BFelements$ elements
passed from the encoder to the cooperative fusion stage.
\item \emph{Quantisation.} Each element is transmitted at $w = \QuantWidth$~bits, giving
$\BFelements \times \QuantWidth = \BFinfobits$ information bits.
\item \emph{Packetisation.} Payloads are cut into $\PacketBits$-bit packets, each carrying a
$\HeaderBits$-bit header: $\BFpackets$ packets and $\BFheaderbits$ header bits in total. The final
packet is short and is padded, which is why the count matters.
\item \emph{Channel coding.} Each packet's bits are encoded with a rate-$1/2$ LDPC code of
information length $K = \LdpcK$ and codeword length $n = \LdpcN$, giving $N_{\mathrm{cw}} = \BFncw$
codewords for the whole message.
\item \emph{Modulation.} At $\QamM$-QAM, $N_{\mathrm{cw}} \times n / \log_2 \QamM$ channel uses give
$B_F = \BFmsym$~Msym per frame.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why the codeword route rather than the direct one.} Computing
$(\text{info} + \text{header}) / R / \log_2 M$ directly gives $\BFdirectmsym$~Msym. The difference
from $\BFmsym$ is codeword padding: the direct route silently assumes the information bits fill an
integer number of codewords. We charge the padding, because the channel does.

The object-level message is charged through the identical chain from each frame's own box count at
$\BoxBits$~bits per box, ranging from $\BLmin$ to $\BLmax$~Msym, i.e.\ $\NcwLmin$ to $\NcwLmax$
codewords. On the development split, the collaborator transmits an average of
$\NboxCollabMean$ boxes per frame, producing
$\NcwLmean$ LDPC codewords and $\BLmean$~Msym. The post-fusion late-detection output contains
$\NboxLateOutputMean$ boxes on average, but this value is not used for payload accounting.
At the tightest budget tier $\beta = \BudgetTightBeta$ the mean object-level payload is
$\BLshareTightPct$\% of the ceiling, which is why the object-level action is effectively free at
every tier studied here.

\textbf{Two AWGN points are marked \emph{squeeze}.} They were absent from the link-level sweep and

\section{The complete transport sweep}
Table~\ref{tab:transport} gives all $\TransportRates$ evaluated per-codeword loss rates in the three
delivery regimes, with $\TransportReplicates$ independent channel realisations per rate averaged
with equal weight. Interpolation between rates is piecewise linear in raw $p$, never in $\log p$:
the damaged fraction of transmitted elements is approximately $p$, and $\log p$ diverges at $p = 0$,
so it would need a special case to cover its own endpoint.

Two readings follow.

\emph{The message column measures the survival probability, not the feature action.} At
$p = \MsgLowestRate$ a $\BFncw$-codeword message survives with probability
$q = \MsgSurvivalAtLowest$, an expectation of $\MsgFramesAtLowest$ surviving frames over the whole
development split. Its AP therefore sits at the ego-only value at every non-zero rate, which is a
statement about message length rather than about feature-level fusion.

\emph{No monotone correction is applied.} Some masks raise a frame's $F_1$ by removing false
positives, and forcing monotonicity would use smoothing to erase a measured effect.

\section{Loss position at fixed loss amount}
Table~\ref{tab:position} gives the loss-position result at every rate. The construction is strict:
pairs of replicates are matched on \emph{exactly equal} codeword-loss counts, so the two members of a
pair lose the same \emph{amount} and differ only in \emph{which} codewords were lost. A non-zero
$\Delta F_1$ within a matched pair therefore isolates position.

The sufficiency rule---minimum pair count, minimum frame count, and a Wilson 95\% lower bound
strictly above zero---was fixed before the selection ran, on a distributional argument about tie
frequency rather than on a look at the outcome column.

\textbf{What this supports and what it does not.} It supports the statement that loss position
affects task performance. It does not support the stronger statement that position matters
\emph{more} than amount: that comparison needs a proportion threshold fixed in advance, and none was
registered. Setting one now would be setting it with the data on screen, so the stronger claim is
left unadjudicated rather than argued.

\section{Exploratory penalty scan}
\textbf{This section is an exploratory diagnostic on the development split.} It was registered
before it produced any number, it enters no primary criterion, and the frozen candidate was
\emph{not} replaced on the strength of it. It is reported because it answers the obvious question
about $\rho_F = 0$, and suppressing it would leave that question answered only by assertion.

Fig.~\ref{fig:lambda} and Table~\ref{tab:lambda} scan $\lambda$ over $\LamScanPoints$ points from
$\LamScanLo$ to $\LamScanHi$. Across that interval $\rho_F$ falls smoothly from $\LamScanRhoFLo$ to
$\LamScanRhoFHi$, so a genuine three-action trade-off exists in the region between the preregistered
grid's zero point and its smallest non-zero point. The frozen candidate's penalty
$\lambda = \LambdaStar$ lies far outside that region, at $\lambda B_F = \LambdaTimesBF$~Msym against
a mean net gain of $\FBestNetGain$ on the rows where F is best---a ratio of
$\PenaltyOverGainRatio$.

\textbf{This is a statement about grid resolution, not about whether the feature action is worth its
cost.} The two must not be merged: the first says no candidate could have selected F, the second
would be a claim about the action itself, and only the first is supported.

\section{Dual payload accounting}
Every payload figure in the main paper is reported under two accountings, and they are never mixed:
over \emph{all} frames of a split, and over only those frames where a collaborator is available.
Frames without a collaborator cost every arm zero, so including them can only shrink absolute
payloads---and could in principle inflate an apparent relative saving.

Measured, it does not. Collaborators are available on \TestCollabShare\% of Test rows and
\CulverCollabShare\% of Culver-City rows, and the relative saving is identical under both
accountings on both splits. The concern was stated in advance and is settled by measurement rather
than carried as a caveat.

\textbf{Note that this is not a second payload convention for the external baseline.} Dual
accounting is full-frame versus collaborator-available frames within \method{}'s single payload
definition; Where2comm is reported on its native communication rate only and enters neither
accounting.

\section{Reproducibility}
\emph{Determinism.} The per-sample point shuffle is seeded on
$(\text{split}, \text{scene}, \text{frame}, \text{cav})$. Before this fix the shuffle drew from a
global unseeded generator, so two runs of the same frame could disagree---by one box on a small
fraction of frames, and by $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$ in AP. The fault was found by a
reconstruction-consistency bridge that compares two independent runs bit for bit; after the fix that
bridge reports zero differing frames and an AP difference of exactly zero. The whole seed identity
set is enumerated for collisions on every run.

\emph{Bootstrap.} Intervals are scene-level bootstraps: scenes are resampled with replacement and
the statistic recomputed, because frames within a scene are consecutive samples of the same traffic
and are far from independent. Frame-level resampling would give intervals that are too narrow by a
large factor. The confirmatory unit is the scene throughout.

\emph{Selection versus evaluation.} Leave-one-scene-out cross-validation on the development split is
the \emph{training-time} procedure that chose the candidate and the penalty; the scene-level
bootstrap is the \emph{evaluation-time} procedure that produced the intervals on the held-out
splits. They act at different stages and neither substitutes for the other.

\emph{Products.} Every number in the main paper and in this document is emitted by one generator
from closed-out result files; none is typed by hand. The generator has a check mode that
re-derives every macro and every table body and fails if the delivered text is not what the products
produce.
